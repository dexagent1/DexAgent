# Stage 2: property-based simulation reconstruction

## Contract

INPUT: the video, Stage 1's `scene_semantics.json`, the setup (`setup/setup.yaml` + `setup/scene.xml`: the table, the two robot stations at their home pose, the calibrated ego camera), and the camera source from the run configuration. For an EgoDex episode the `.hdf5` is given too.

OUTPUT, in the workspace:
- `scene.xml`: the setup scene with every reconstructed rigid and articulated object attached at the pose the video shows, compiling, physically stable, with the video camera written in as camera `video_cam`. Deformable objects are not in it: they are simulated in Isaac Sim (step 9), except a cord that Stage 1 cuts.
- `camera.json`: the video camera in the rig frame (from `register_table_camera` or `read_egodex_episode`).
- `masks/<id>.png` per object and `masks/hands.png`, at the video resolution.
- `assets/<id>/`: each object's asset as built by its reconstruction skill.
- for every deformable object: its Isaac Sim reconstruction from `build_isaac_scene` (`isaac/`): the cord laid along its measured centreline and settled in PhysX on the same bench, its render, and `isaac/<tag>_<name>_cord.json`, the cord's specification with its settled link poses, which Stage 3 executes (`execute_isaac_deformables`) and Stage 4 replays. It needs Isaac Sim's Python (`environments.isaac`).
- `frame0_compare.png` from `render_frame_compare`: the video frame, the render through the same camera, their blend (and the Isaac render when there is one).
- `scene_recon.json`, the manifest (schema below).

Stage 3 drives the robot against exactly this scene: an object with a missing joint cannot be opened, a wrong size cannot be grasped the way the human did, a pose off by centimetres sends the hand to the wrong place. Properties and placement are the whole job.

The initial reference is source video frame 0. Later frames may explain a
mechanism or reveal an occluded part, but cannot replace the initial reference
or its masks. Object masks must be nonempty, except for an object declared hidden at frame 0 (step 3). The optional hands/arms exclusion
mask must come from an approved direct SAM call with only hand/arm prompts; an
empty result is valid when no hands are visible. Do not fabricate a nonempty
exclusion with a CPU box or rename an object mask as the hands mask.

Stored mechanism reports propose checks; their `ok` fields cannot certify the scene.
The current independent adapter replays `articulated_joint_travel_test` for passive,
uncoupled, limited hinge/slide components using fixed defaults and exact local
asset/scene physics binding. It proves isolated fixed-base component mobility,
not environment clearance or robot feasibility. Other articulated mechanism
claims, including newly created verifiers, remain unsupported for acceptance
until the framework supplies an independent adapter. Report that limit rather
than dropping a required property or replacing it with an unrelated travel test.

## Method (the paper's procedure)

0. **Use the current inputs.** Reconstruct from the supplied video and Stage 1 description using parameterized library tools. If the workspace already contains a reconstruction, treat it as an unverified candidate: check its object identities, properties, measurements and physical behavior against these inputs, repair discrepancies, and rerun all required checks. Do not inspect other runs or solutions for this sample. Measure parameters and placement from the current demonstration.
1. **Read Stage 1.** For each object note its type, parts, the properties the reconstruction must show, and which subgoals use it (what the object must be able to do). An object with `pieces` is a group: `pieces.count` separate pieces of one `kind` (a tower of loose blocks, a pile or fan of cards) under one id and one mask.
2. **Camera.** Our bench videos: `register_table_camera(source='setup', frame=...)`; look at `camera_check.png`: the 10 cm grid must lie on the cloth and its lines must converge with the table edges. EgoDex: `read_egodex_episode` writes `camera.json` and the frames from the episode's own intrinsics and pose; its head camera has no fixed place on our bench, so once the object masks exist call `place_egodex_camera_for_reach(out_dir, episode_dir=<read_egodex_episode out_dir>, masks='masks', objects=[the objects the hands manipulate], exclude=[parts resting on another object], flat=[the deformables lying on the table])`: it picks the camera's foot and yaw so every object sits at least 3 cm inside an arm's palm-down reach band (checked by exact IK), and writes the re-framed `camera.json`, `hands.npz` and `episode.json` to use from then on. Never sweep `cam_xy` by hand. A video from an unknown camera: `estimate_gravity` (pitch, roll) and `estimate_depth` + `fit_camera_to_table_plane` (height) then `register_table_camera(source='estimate', ...)`; check the grid again. When the demonstrator's hands rest where the robot's hands start (setup home) in an early frame, `register_camera_hand_overlay(frame, masks/hands.png, 'camera.json', init=<the estimate>, table_prior=<gravity / table-plane fit>)` refines yaw, foot and height from the overlay of the robot's home hands on the human hands; it reports `accepted: false` below IoU 0.7 (on our bench footage the resting hands sit about 10 cm from home) and then the table-plane camera stays. The run configuration may give the camera (`source='given'`). When the grid does not sit on the table, nothing downstream can.
3. **Frame and masks.** Call `extract_frames` and `segment_with_text_prompts` directly as harness tools on the original video for source frame 0. Use each object's Stage 1 name and `['human hand', 'arm', 'sleeve']` for the hands. For object masks without SAM, `segment_source_frame(video, frame_index=0, box, out_dir)` computes a CPU GrabCut mask and a lossless source frame; inspect these and revise the box when needed. A CPU box does not certify a hands exclusion mask. Point `scene_recon.frame0` to the matching source frame. Copy masks byte for byte to `masks/<id>.png` and `masks/hands.png`; do not paint them, substitute simulator renders, or modify their pixels. All reference masks, including the hands exclusion, require harness receipts tied to the same source frame. A written provenance file or a call inside `run_python` cannot certify one. A fresh process independently replays supported CPU and SAM recipes, requiring exact source pixels and mask bytes; other legacy masks must be regenerated through the approved tools. Source provenance is separate from segmentation accuracy: inspect masks over the source image and retry poor segmentations before fitting the scene. An object the task uses that is not visible at all at frame 0 (a watch inside a closed box, a part wholly covered by another object or the hand) is still segmented on source frame 0 through the approved path, and that empty result is its `mask`; segment the first frame k that reveals it the same way for `masks/<id>_reveal.png`, place it where the reveal and the demonstration put it, and declare `"hidden_at_frame0": {"reason": "<what covers it>", "reveal_frame": k, "reveal_mask": "masks/<id>_reveal.png"}` in its entry. Instead of an IoU, `scene_qc` then requires it to render (near) invisible through `video_cam` at keyframe 0 while lying in the view; never declare a visible object hidden, and never use a later frame's mask as a frame-0 mask.
4. **Verification plan first, per object (you decide it).** Before building anything, write for each object which checks will prove each Stage 1 property and the placement, and record it in the manifest as `verification_plan`. Search the verifiers (`library_search` with type verifier) and pick: rigid: `silhouette_iou_check` after placement, `settle_stability_check`, `support_check`, `color_match_check`, `vessel_containment_test` for cups and bowls, `box_enclosure_check` for every box, tray, drawer, bin or lidded container (a bottom, four walls and a hollow inside, with `expect_top` as frame 0 shows it), `height_check` / `above_check` for stacked or nested objects; articulated: `articulation_check` plus the mechanism's own test (`thread_self_locking_test` for a screw cap, `articulated_joint_travel_test` for drawers and hinges, `latch_snap_test`, `flip_cap_snap_test`, `fold_box_fold_test`, `scissors_cut_test`, `battery_seat_test`, `spring_stiffness_test`, `plunger_two_stage_test`, `tube_seat_test`, `ink_round_trip_test`) and `joint_range_check` when the video shows how far a part moves; deformable: `measure_rope`'s radius constancy and `isaac_deformable_rest_check` (the settled cord: drift, connectivity, self-overlap, table, placement); for a cut cord (a MuJoCo cable) `cable_drag_test`. When a property has no verifier in the library (a trigger that must pump, a lid that must stay open at an angle, two parts that must nest), write one with `library_create`: it returns ok, issues and the measurement, it is tested on three variations, and it joins the library like any other tool. The plan is yours; required claims also face the independent checks described above. Saved reports and property mappings alone cannot certify a physical claim.
5. **Reconstruction skill, per object.** `library_search` the Object Reconstruction skills with the object's name, type and parts (there are constructors for bottles with screw caps, cups, bowls, boxes with hinged lids, latch boxes, drawers and cabinets through URDF, scissors, ropes, springs, batteries and holders, markers, erasers, whiteboards, hard disks, racks, cases, lab tubes, racks, pipettes and tips, cubes, and any mesh). An existing skill is always preferred: read its `DEFAULTS`, set the parameters from measurements (step 6) and the Stage 1 properties (part counts, joint type, what moves), and build into `assets/<id>/`. Only when no skill covers the object's mechanism, create one with `library_create`, a parameterized function for the class of object, built property by property: (a) start from an editable description of the object with no joints (URDF through `construct_articulated` / `port_urdf_asset`, or MJCF) at the measured size; (b) for each Stage 1 property in turn, first choose or write the verifier that proves it, then add the joint, part or contact parameter that makes it pass, and move on only when it passes; (c) run all the object's verifiers together, so a later property has not broken an earlier one; (d) a second round: add verifiers for what the first round did not test (limits, robustness, the hand's interaction), and build three more parameter variants that pass every verifier. The tool's test is that last round. To change an existing skill, fork it under a new name; never edit it.
6. **Measurements.** `measure_mask_footprint(mask, camera.json, ignore_mask=masks/hands.png)`: the contact centre on the table (initial x, y), the width across the view and the apparent height (sizes for the constructor); for flat objects the footprint length, width and yaw. `measure_object_color` for the colours. `measure_box_on_table` for box-like objects. Known standard objects (an AA cell, a letter-size board) take their true dimensions; the footprint width is the check.
7. **Build and verify each asset.** Call the constructor; run its verifiers; adjust parameters until they pass. The asset's XML must have `include_floor` off or the bench strips it; the object is a free body unless the task holds it fixed. A group object (Stage 1 `pieces`) is built as `pieces.count` separate bodies, each staged under the world and named with the object's id prefix, each with its own free joint when Stage 1 says the pieces are loose; never weld loose pieces together, and never list a piece as an object of its own when Stage 1 does not (that is a Stage 1 revision). In its entry, `parts` lists every piece's root body and `root_body` is one of them: the piece the first contact subgoal acts on.
8. **Place.** `stage_on_bench(setup/scene.xml, assets=[{name: id, xml, x, y, yaw_deg, joints: {joint: value}}], out_xml='scene.xml', rig={'origin': [0,0,0], 'R': identity}, cameras={'video_cam': 'camera.json'})`: the setup scene's world frame is the rig frame, so the rig must be given explicitly; `joints` sets each asset's initial joint values as frame 0 shows them (a lid open at 190 degrees, a drawer pulled 5 cm), so an existing constructor serves even when its default state differs from the video; `cameras` writes the video camera into the scene for the checks. The scene's initial state is its keyframe 0, which every verifier and render uses. Then `fit_object_placement` per object: `x, y, yaw` for objects standing on the table, add `z, pitch, roll` for a lying or leaning object, add `scale` when the size came from an estimate (then rebuild the asset at the fitted size and fit again); pass `masks/hands.png` as ignore_mask; write `out_xml` back to `scene.xml` each time. Fit the large and unoccluded objects first; an object inside another (a cup in a bowl) after its container. Objects that touch in the video must touch in the scene without interpenetrating: `penetration_check`, then `settle_stability_check` and `support_check` on the whole scene. An object lying flipped or on its side (a phone screen up, a bottle on its side) takes `roll_deg` / `pitch_deg` (or a full `quat`) in its asset entry, never a hand edit of scene.xml. A connector the video plugs in (a USB-C plug into a phone) gets its latch once the placements are final: `add_connector_latch(scene.xml, plug_body, plug_site, port_body, port_site)` writes the latch weld into scene.xml (the plug's `plug.tip_site` and the port's `port_floor` site, with their scene prefixes), and its `scene_fields` (`latch`) go into the plug's entry, so the Stage 3 executor holds the seated plug the way the real latch does.
9. **Deformables (Isaac Sim).** Deformable objects are reconstructed and simulated in Isaac Sim, never as MuJoCo assets. `measure_rope(mask, camera)` gives the centreline in metres (through `common.rigcam.to_tablecam` when the camera is a camera.json) and the radius. Once the rigid objects are placed (the export takes scene.xml as it is), `build_isaac_scene(scene.xml, camera.json, deformables=[{name, points, radius, rgb, preset, ...}], out_dir='isaac')` lays the cord on the same bench, settles it and writes the stage, the render, the report and `isaac/isaac_<name>_cord.json`. Choose `preset` from the cord: `thin_cord` (the default: a thread, a string, a cable of a few millimetres) or `segmented_rope` (a thick heavy rope, such as a velvet stanchion rope); `cap_rgb` with `cap_length_a_m` / `cap_length_b_m` for rigid end fittings; `physics` overrides only with a reason. Read the drift: the cord must stay where it was laid. The entry gets `"simulator": "isaac"`, `"skill": "build_isaac_scene"`, no `asset_xml` or `root_body`, `"simulation_model": {"kind": "isaac_cord", "approximation": "segmented capsule chain"}`, `pose` = the centreline's centroid with yaw 0, and the `isaac` block with `cord_json` (schema below); the scene's `simulator` is `"mujoco+isaac"`, and scene.xml must not contain a MuJoCo cable for it. `isaac_deformable_rest_check(scene.xml, scene_recon.json)` re-measures the cord from those files (drift from the lay, one connected chain, no self-overlap, nothing sunk into the table or an object, no MuJoCo copy) and scores the settled cord's silhouette against its mask by centreline: that score is the entry's `iou`. Never fix a cord end (`fix_start` / `fix_end` weld it to the world). Stage 3 executes the robot in MuJoCo and the cord in Isaac Sim (`execute_isaac_deformables`). This supports cords, not cloth or volumetric soft bodies. The exception is cutting, which Isaac Sim does not have yet: a cord that Stage 1 CUTS is a MuJoCo cable. Build it with `construct_cuttable_rope` (the measured centreline, the cut where the video cuts it), stage it at x = y = z = 0, copy its `scene_fields` (root_body, bodies, halves, cut_joins) into the entry, record the scissors' `cutter` (construct_scissors' output) in theirs, and declare `simulation_model: {"kind": "mujoco_cable", "approximation": "discrete elastic cable"}`; `cable_drag_test`, `deformable_rest_check` (link drifts, contacts, simulator warnings) and `deformable_topology_check` (connectivity, self-contact) prove it. Such a cable is held to one chain with two free ends; one of another shape, which Stage 1 describes (a ribbon of three strands joined at one end, a loop), declares it in its entry as `"topology": {"strands": 1, "ends": 3}` (ends 0 for a loop). A cable end the video shows held under a fixed object (a thread under a tape dispenser) may be welded to that object's body (`anchor_cord_end_to_body`; anchor the cord by its root link, a weld on a deep link of a nested cable sags) when the entry declares `"anchored_to": {"body": "<fixed object's body>", "reason": "<the Stage 1 property>"}`; a weld to the world, the table or the robot is never allowed.
10. **Check the whole scene.** `scene_qc(scene.xml, required=[root bodies and every group's parts], articulated=[...], masks={root body, or a group's id: mask}, groups={group id: its parts}, camera='video_cam')`; `render_frame_compare` to `frame0_compare.png`; look at it: the blend must show single outlines, not doubles. `stage2_gate_suite` independently checks conservative kinematic workspace bounds for the Stage 1 acting arms, manipulated objects and destinations (`object_reach_check`). An object outside these necessary bounds blocks the handoff; failed finite pose probes are reported as inconclusive: re-check the camera registration, metric scale and placement without changing known calibration or the supplied robot mounts. If the calibrated physical setup cannot reach it, report the incompatibility; a warning in the manifest cannot certify an executable scene. This geometric check is a prerequisite; Stage 3 must still solve actual contact poses and collision-free paths.
11. **Write `scene_recon.json`**, run `scene_recon_manifest_check` yourself, fix what it reports, call `finish`.

## Rules

- Reuse before build. The library's constructors are parameterized for exactly this; a new skill is for a mechanism none of them has (and then it joins the library with its tests).
- Every property from Stage 1 maps to a parameter and a verifier, or to a stated reason it is not reproduced. Record the mapping in `properties_covered`, and the checks you chose (and built) in `verification_plan`.
- Sizes come from measurements, not from guesses: the footprint width and height, the standard size of a known object, the depth. Colours come from `measure_object_color` on the mask.
- The robot setup is fixed: never move the stations, the table or the home pose. Place objects on the table (z = 0 at the footprint) unless they rest on another object.
- Objects that the task moves are free bodies; objects the video shows as fixed to the table stay fixed.
- The hands are not in the scene; their pixels are ignored in every fit (`ignore_mask`).
- An object part that moves in the video (a lid, a cap, a drawer) must be a joint in the asset, and the joint's initial state must match frame 0 (open lid open, cap on): set it with `joints` in `stage_on_bench`, not by writing a new constructor.
- Mechanism tests run on the asset alone: `thread_self_locking_test` and `articulated_joint_travel_test` hold a free root body themselves, `vessel_containment_test` adds a floor when the asset has none. `silhouette_iou_check` scores an object as its root body with everything hinged on it, and a group object (given in `groups`) as the union of its pieces against its one mask. `penetration_check` on the whole scene reports pairs between the robot's own hands at the home pose; judge the objects' pairs.
- Keep the budget: one camera, one mask pass, one build per object (plus retries driven by a failing verifier), placement fits in order, one Isaac run per deformable.

`iou` is `silhouette_iou_check`'s score for the object (a thin object, a cord or a lying pen, is scored by its centreline, since its mask is wider than the object; a deformable in Isaac Sim takes `isaac_deformable_rest_check`'s score of its settled cord; a group object by the union of its pieces; pass the hands' mask as `ignore_mask` where they cover it); a group object (Stage 1 `pieces`) adds `"parts": ["<root body of each piece>", ...]`, which `scene_recon_manifest_check` holds to Stage 1's count, the free-joint check exempts from the tie-to-the-object rule, and the contact check measures to the nearest piece; `simulation_model` names the verified deformable simulation representation: `isaac_cord` with the `isaac` block for a deformable simulated in Isaac Sim, `mujoco_cable` for a cut cord (and `topology` for a cable that is not one chain); a rope that a subgoal cuts also carries `cut_joins` and `halves` (from `construct_cuttable_rope`'s `scene_fields`), and scissors carry `cutter`. On `finish` the harness runs `scene_recon_manifest_check` (bookkeeping: every object, type, skill, asset, body, pose, mask, IoU, verifier reports; for a deformable in Isaac Sim its cord specification with settled poses, a clean settling report, its files and no MuJoCo copy; for a cut cord a MuJoCo cable with its cut joins), `scene_qc` (compile, units, penetration, settling, support, required bodies, articulation, silhouette IoU through `video_cam`) and `stage2_gate_suite` (the robot, its actuators, joint ranges, contacts, stations and the physics options are exactly the setup's; keyframe 0 is a settled state; masses, inertias and frictions are plausible; every object is in the camera's view, built to the size its mask shows, typed and counted as Stage 1 says; for an EgoDex episode, the demonstrator's fingertips reach each object where they first touch it; the scene compiles and simulates stably; a deformable in Isaac Sim is re-measured from its files by `isaac_deformable_rest_check`). Run `stage2_gate_suite(workspace, scene_semantic_json)` yourself before `finish`. Those are the floor; the verifiers that prove the properties are the ones you planned and ran.
